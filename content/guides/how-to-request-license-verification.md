## Why Verification Comes First

Almost every interstate license application requires the receiving state to confirm your license is real, active, and free of disciplinary action. This step is called license verification, and the receiving board usually will not finish reviewing your application until it arrives.

## Step 1: Identify the Verification Method Your Profession Uses

Some professions verify through a national registry — nursing uses [Nursys](https://www.nursys.com/), the official verification database operated by the National Council of State Boards of Nursing (NCSBN); most skilled trades require a direct request to the issuing state's licensing board, often through an online portal or a signed paper form.

## Step 2: Request Early

Request verification before you submit your full application to the new state. Verification is handled by your current state's board or by Nursys, separately from the receiving state's own review, and the boards we cite do not publish a turnaround time for it.

## What the Boards We Cite Actually Ask For

The exact document differs by board. These are the verification requirements as each board states them on the page we cite:

- **Texas, electricians (TDLR).** A letter of good standing from the issuing state's licensing authority, confirming you passed the statewide electrical exam, hold a current Journeyman Electrician license, and are in good standing. Source: [TDLR out-of-state electrician page](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/out-of-state.htm).
- **New York, registered nurses (NYSED).** Form 1 (the application), Form 2 sent directly by your nursing school, and Form 3 or Nursys verification of your license. Source: [NYSED endorsement page](https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/registered-professional-nursing/endorsement-nursing-licenses).
- **California, registered nurses (BRN).** Nursys or direct license verification, plus official transcripts sent directly from your nursing school. Source: [BRN licensure by endorsement](https://www.rn.ca.gov/applicants/lic-end).
- **Illinois, registered nurses (IDFPR).** License verification through Nursys when your current state participates in it, as Georgia does. Source: [IDFPR RN endorsement application](https://idfpr.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/idfpr/renewals/apply/forms/RN-EN.pdf).

Two patterns show up across these: the three nursing boards accept Nursys verification, and the Texas trade board asks for a letter from the issuing board confirming your license and exam history.

## Step 3: Track the Delivery Method

Many boards send verification directly to the receiving state rather than to you. Confirm whether you need to provide the receiving state's mailing address or portal ID as part of the request.

## Step 4: Follow Up Before Assuming It's Lost

If you haven't seen confirmation within the state's stated processing window, follow up directly — verification requests are one of the most common points where paperwork silently stalls.
