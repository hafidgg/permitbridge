## Why Verification Comes First

When you apply in a new state, the receiving board checks that your existing license is real, active and in good standing. This step is called license verification, and each board we cite below asks for it.

## Step 1: Identify the Verification Method Your Profession Uses

Some professions verify through a national registry: nursing uses [Nursys](https://www.nursys.com/), the verification database operated by the National Council of State Boards of Nursing (NCSBN). Trade boards may instead ask for a letter from the issuing board, as Texas's TDLR does for electricians (below).

## Step 2: Request Early

Request verification before you submit your full application to the new state. Verification is handled by your current state's board or by Nursys, separately from the receiving state's own review, and the boards we cite do not publish a turnaround time for it.

## What the Boards We Cite Actually Ask For

The exact document differs by board. These are the verification requirements as each board states them on the page we cite:

- **Texas, electricians (TDLR).** A letter of good standing from the issuing state's licensing authority, confirming you passed the statewide electrical exam, hold a current Journeyman Electrician license, and are in good standing. Source: [TDLR out-of-state electrician page](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/out-of-state.htm).
- **New York, registered nurses (NYSED).** Form 1 (the application), Form 2 (Certification of Professional Education), and Form 3 or Nursys verification of your license. Source: [NYSED endorsement page](https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/registered-professional-nursing/endorsement-nursing-licenses).
- **California, registered nurses (BRN).** Nursys or direct license verification, plus transcripts sent to the board electronically through a transcript vendor or directly from your school. Source: [BRN licensure by endorsement](https://www.rn.ca.gov/applicants/lic-end).
- **Illinois, registered nurses (IDFPR).** License verification through Nursys when your current state participates in it, as Georgia does. Source: [IDFPR RN endorsement application](https://idfpr.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/idfpr/renewals/apply/forms/RN-EN.pdf).

Two patterns show up across these: the three nursing boards accept Nursys verification, and the Texas trade board asks for a letter from the issuing board confirming your license and exam history.

## Step 3: Track the Delivery Method

Some documents go directly to the receiving board rather than through you; California's BRN, for example, takes transcripts electronically from a transcript vendor or directly from your school. Confirm whether you need to give the receiving board's mailing address or portal ID as part of the request.

## Step 4: Follow Up Before Assuming It's Lost

If the receiving board hasn't confirmed that your verification arrived, contact it to check, and ask your current board to confirm it was sent.
