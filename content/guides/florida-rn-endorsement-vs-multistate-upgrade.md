## What This Guide Covers

The Florida Board of Nursing describes two different applications that are easy to confuse: licensure by endorsement, which it calls MOBILE Endorsement, and the Multi-state Upgrade. This guide sets out what the board's own pages and forms say about each: who can use it, what it requires, and what it costs.

It covers registered nurses (RNs); the board applies the same pages to licensed practical nurses (LPNs). It does not cover licensure by examination, nurses educated outside the U.S. in detail, or advanced practice (APRN) licensure. It is not legal advice.

## What the Board's Own Text Says

### The short version

The two applications serve different people:

- **MOBILE Endorsement** is for a nurse licensed in another state. The board's [RN and LPN page](https://floridasnursing.gov/nurse-rn-lpn/) says applicants "Must hold an active, unencumbered license issued by another state, the District of Columbia, or a territory of the United States".
- **Multi-state Upgrade** is for a nurse who already holds a Florida license. The [upgrade application](https://floridasnursing.gov/applications/multistate-upgrade-app.pdf) states: "To apply for a multistate license upgrade you must have a current single state Florida Licensed Practical Nurse (LPN) or Registered Nurse (RN) license."

### MOBILE Endorsement requirements

The board lists the requirements of section 456.0145, Florida Statutes. They include:

- "Must hold an active, unencumbered license issued by another state, the District of Columbia, or a territory of the United States in a profession with a similar scope of practice"
- "Must have obtained a passing score on a national licensure examination or hold a national certification recognized by the board"
- "Must have actively practiced the profession for at least two years during the four-year period immediately preceding the date of submission of this application."
- "Must not have had disciplinary action taken in the five years immediately preceding the date of submission of the application."

For nurses without two years of practice, the page notes: "if you do not have two years of active practice, you may apply by Examination and transfer your passing NCLEX scores from NCSBN."

The board's [endorsement checklist](https://floridasnursing.gov/forms/sd-nursing-endorsement-transaction.pdf) adds: "Verification of every license you hold or have held is required. You may request verification of your license at www.nursys.com or by contacting the state or jurisdiction where you were issued each license." It also requires fingerprints: "All applicants are required to be background screened," and says to use "the Florida Board of Nursing ORI number when getting your prints taken- EDOH4420Z."

### MOBILE Endorsement fee and timing

The RN and LPN page lists "$110.00 Application and Licensing Fee to Board of Nursing", plus a fee that "varies" for the Livescan provider and for license verification.

On timing it states: "Please allow up to 10 business days for initial review of new applications." It adds: "When all requirements are met the license will be issued to qualified within 7 days pursuant to Section 456.0145(2)(e), Florida Statutes." The board's general [licensing page](https://floridasnursing.gov/licensing/) states: "The application process may take between two to six months to complete," and "If anything is deficient, you will receive a deficiency letter within approximately 30 days after the receipt of your application."

### Multi-state Upgrade requirements

The RN and LPN page lists the upgrade requirements under section 464.0095, Florida Statutes, including:

- "You must hold a Clear, Active, Florida license."
- "You must declare Florida your primary state of residency."
- "Livescan results must be submitted no more than 90 days prior to the submission of the application and fees or anytime within a year of applying."
- "You must not have a felony conviction."
- "Must have passed the NCLEX or the SBTPE."

The application adds that a nurse whose home is in another compact state cannot use it: "If your declared primary state of residency is another Compact State, you are not eligible for a multistate license in Florida." It also states: "A nurse may only hold one multistate license."

### Multi-state Upgrade fee

The application states: "Total fee of $100.00 includes the following: ... Initial Licensure Fee $50.00." It explains that "An applicant who is denied licensure or withdraws their application is entitled to a $50.00 (Initial Licensure Fee) refund." It also notes that the application "is separate from a renewal and will not change your expiration date or license number."

### What the compact means here

The upgrade application states that the Nurse Licensure Compact "allows a registered nurse or licensed practical nurse licensed in a Compact State to practice across state lines in another Compact State without having to obtain a license in the other state unless the nurse moves and declares the new Compact State as their new primary state of residence." It adds: "If a nurse moves from one state to another and establishes residency, the nurse must apply for licensure in that state."

## Steps, as the Board Describes Them

For MOBILE Endorsement, the RN and LPN page lists:

1. "Contact the state board(s) in which a license has been held to request License Verification be sent to Florida."
2. "Transfer National Examination scores from NCSBN."
3. "Apply online at www.flhealthsource.gov, pay applicable fees, and upload any supplemental documentation."
4. "Schedule an electronic fingerprint appointment and be printed."

For the Multi-state Upgrade: confirm you hold a clear, active Florida license, that Florida is your primary state of residence, and that your Livescan results fall within the window above, then submit the upgrade application with the $100.00 fee.

## What This Guide Cannot Tell You

Confirm these with the Florida Board of Nursing before you apply:

- Which application to file if you hold a multistate license from another compact state and are moving to Florida. The upgrade form requires a current Florida license, and the sources we read do not name the application for that situation directly.
- How long your application will take. The board publishes an initial review window, a seven-day issuance rule once requirements are met, and a general two-to-six-month range.
- What the Livescan and license verification fees will cost you.
- How a criminal, health or disciplinary history affects your application, which the board reviews individually.
