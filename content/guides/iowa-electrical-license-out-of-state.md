## What This Guide Covers

This guide sets out what the Iowa Electrical Examining Board, part of the Department of Inspections, Appeals, and Licensing (DIAL), publishes for electricians who already hold a license in another state. It covers the reciprocal agreements, the conditions in Iowa's administrative rules, the route for out-of-state electricians from states without an agreement, the license fees, continuing education, and how to apply.

It covers journeyman and master Class A licenses. It does not cover residential, special or Class B licenses in detail, electrical contractor requirements, or permits. Our [journeyman reciprocity guide](/guides/journeyman-electrician-license-reciprocity) compares Iowa's journeyman agreements with Texas and Minnesota; this guide covers Iowa's own rules more fully.

## What the Board's Own Text Says

### The states Iowa has agreements with

DIAL's [reciprocal licensing agreements document](https://dial.iowa.gov/media/7486/download?inline=), dated 1/29/2026, states: "The State of Iowa Electrical Examining Board has reciprocal licensing agreements with Alaska Arkansas, Colorado, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, Wisconsin and Wyoming."

It sets out the conditions in one sentence: "The applicant must have passed an approved State-sponsored examination with a score of 70% or greater, held the qualifying license for 1 year which cannot have been expired, suspended, or revoked; cannot have taken the Iowa exam and failed it; and must meet additional Iowa licensure requirements in order for the license to qualify for reciprocation."

For journeyman licenses, every listed state's journeyman license "may reciprocate to an Iowa Journeyman Class A license". For master licenses the Iowa license depends on the state:

| Your license | Iowa license the document names |
|---|---|
| Arkansas Master, Texas Master Electrician | Master Class A |
| Nebraska Electrical Contractor | Master Class A |
| Minnesota Class A Master, North Dakota Class A Master | Master or Journeyman Class A |
| Wisconsin Master Electrician, Wyoming Master Electrician | Master or Journeyman Class A |
| South Dakota Electrical Contractor | Journeyman or Master Class A |
| Alaska, Colorado, Montana or New Hampshire Master | Journeyman Class A |
| Oklahoma Electrical Contractor | Journeyman Class A |

DIAL's FAQ page links this document with the label "Archived".

### What Iowa's rules add

Iowa Administrative Code [chapter 481—401](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/chapter/08-06-2025.481.401.pdf) sets out the reciprocal licensing conditions. Rule 401.2(14) states: "A journeyman class A license may be issued, without examination, to a person who holds a license from another state provided that" the board has an agreement with that state and the applicant meets the listed conditions. Rule 401.2(15) does the same for "A master class A license".

Both rules require that "The applicant has successfully completed a supervised written examination approved by the other state with a score of 70 or higher". Both require that the applicant "has held the applicable license from the other state continuously for one year at the time the application for an Iowa license is filed". Both also require that the applicant has either:

- "Completed an approved apprenticeship program; or"
- "Completed 16,000 hours of electrical work as an electrician licensed by the other state"

For journeyman applicants, the hours are "documented by submission of a sworn affidavit signed by the applicant." The application must include "A copy of the applicable license from the other state, clearly showing the license number and any other identifying information".

### If your state has no agreement

Rule 401.2(6) lists ways to obtain a journeyman Class A license. One applies to a person who "Holds a current electrician license in another state, has passed a supervised written examination for journeyman electrician approved by the board with a score of 70 or higher within 24 months of submission of a new application". That person must also provide evidence of:

- "Current licensure as a journeyman or master electrician from another state which required passing a test sponsored by that state."
- "Completion of 18 hours of continuing education units approved by the board."
- "Completion of 1,000 hours of work in Iowa as an unclassified person."

DIAL also publishes a [License by Verification Form](https://dial.iowa.gov/media/7427/download?inline) for the other state's licensing body to complete. It states: "All items below must be completed by transferring jurisdiction and sent directly back to the Iowa Electrical Examining Board from the transferring jurisdiction." Among other things, it asks: "Was the license obtained by exam?" The form must be notarized. DIAL's licensing page lists this form with the label "Archived".

### Fees and continuing education

Rule 401.3 sets the license fees. A Journeyman Electrician, Class A license has a 3-year term and a fee of $75. A Master Electrician, Class A license has a 3-year term and a fee of $375. The rule adds: "If a license is issued for less than the period of time specified in the table above, the fee will be prorated according to the number of months for which the license is issued." DIAL's [license fee schedule](https://dial.iowa.gov/media/7431/download?inline) lists the prorated amounts for the 2026-2028 cycle.

[Chapter 481—403](https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/chapter/08-06-2025.481.403.pdf) states: "Each holder of a three-year license is to complete 18 hours of continuing education approved by the board between the time of issuance of the license and prior to issuance of a renewal license."

### Lawful presence

DIAL's [electrical licensing page](https://dial.iowa.gov/licenses/building/electrical-licensing) states: "Iowa licensure requires U.S. citizenship or lawful presence verification through SAVE."

## Steps, as the Forms Describe Them

DIAL's [application page](https://dial.iowa.gov/licenses/building/electrical-licensing/how-do-i-apply-electrical) describes the online process:

1. Go to iowaelectrical.gov and open the "Electrical Licensing System".
2. Create an account as a new user, confirm your email, and log in.
3. Choose "Submit new Application or Renew Existing License" and "Complete the Checklist" by answering the questions.
4. Submit the application. The page states: "If it is accepted, we will send you an email requesting payment. If we need additional information, we will send you an email with what is needed."
5. Pay, after which the page says DIAL will "send you an email with a copy of the license."

For reciprocity, the rules also require a copy of your other-state license and, for the experience condition, proof of an apprenticeship or a sworn affidavit of 16,000 hours.

## What This Guide Cannot Tell You

Confirm these with the Iowa Electrical Examining Board before you apply:

- Whether the reciprocal agreements document and the License by Verification Form are current. DIAL labels both "Archived", although the agreements document is dated 1/29/2026.
- How to choose reciprocity in the online system. The application page names only "Examination", "Apprentice Program" and "Proof of Work Experience".
- The exact prorated fee for the month you apply.
- How long the board takes to process an application. None of the sources we read states a processing time.
- What "additional Iowa licensure requirements" the agreements document refers to, beyond those in the rules.
