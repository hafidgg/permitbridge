## How to Read This Guide

This guide does not summarize reciprocity across the country. It sets out what three official pages say: the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR), the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry (DLI), and the Iowa Electrical Examining Board. One Ohio form is included because it says something specific about licenses obtained through reciprocity. For any other state, go to that state's licensing board.

## Texas (TDLR)

TDLR's page [Transfer Your Out-of-State Electrician License to Texas](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/out-of-state.htm) states: "Texas has reciprocal licensing agreements with the states listed below". For journeymen, the list is:

"Journeyman Electrician - Alabama, Alaska, Arkansas, Idaho, Iowa, Nebraska, New Mexico, Oklahoma, South Dakota and Wyoming"

The page lists three requirements for a Journeyman Electrician:

- "Taken and passed the reciprocal state’s examination"
- "Held the reciprocal license for at least one year"
- "Have at least 8,000 hours under the supervision of a master electrician."

For each listed state, the application items for a journeyman include a completed Journeyman Electrician by Reciprocity License Application Form, a "License fee of $30.00" and a "Copy of your state-issued license".

Texas keeps a separate Master Electrician list, which differs from the journeyman list. For example, it includes "Iowa (Class A master electrician)" and "Ohio", and it excludes Alaska. The page also states: "Other License Types - Currently there are no reciprocal licensing agreements for any other license types."

## Minnesota (DLI)

Minnesota's [electrical license reciprocity page](https://www.dli.mn.gov/workers/electrician-or-electrical-installer/electrical-license-reciprocity) uses the term "journeyworker" for its journeyman-level license. It states: "Applicants may obtain a Minnesota journeyworker electrician license without examination if they obtained their journeyworker electrician license by examination in any of the following states:"

The states listed are Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Iowa, Montana, Nebraska, North Dakota, South Dakota and Wyoming. The page adds: "Applicants that have the equivalent of a Minnesota master electrician license in these states may also obtain the Minnesota journeyworker electrician license under the agreements."

Under "Requirements for reciprocity", the page lists:

- "The applicant must not have failed the same or greater class of license examination in Minnesota."
- "The applicant must have obtained the same or greater class of license in the first state by examination at the state level (the license must not have been obtained through a reciprocal agreement with a city or other political subdivision)."
- "The applicant must have held the license for at least one year in the first state."
- "The applicant's license must be current and in good standing in the first state."
- "The applicant must not be under any order to deny, suspend, or revoke any class of electrical license in Minnesota."
- "The applicant must submit an application and examination fee of $50 with the application."
- "Upon approval, the applicant must submit the required license fee to be issued the license applied for."

The page also covers former Minnesota license holders: "Minnesota electrician license holders that have not renewed their license prior to two years after expiration do not qualify to acquire the same class of license through the reciprocal agreements and must retake and pass the license examination to acquire the license."

## Iowa (Electrical Examining Board)

Iowa's [reciprocal licensing agreements document](https://dial.iowa.gov/media/7486/download?inline=) states: "The State of Iowa Electrical Examining Board has reciprocal licensing agreements with Alaska Arkansas, Colorado, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, New Hampshire, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Texas, Wisconsin and Wyoming."

It sets out the conditions in one sentence: "The applicant must have passed an approved State-sponsored examination with a score of 70% or greater, held the qualifying license for 1 year which cannot have been expired, suspended, or revoked; cannot have taken the Iowa exam and failed it; and must meet additional Iowa licensure requirements in order for the license to qualify for reciprocation."

Iowa then lists each journeyman agreement in both directions. Two examples:

- "Texas Journeyman license may reciprocate to an Iowa Journeyman Class A license, or an Iowa Journeyman Class A license may reciprocate to a Texas Journeyman license."
- "Minnesota Class A Journeyman license may reciprocate to an Iowa Journeyman Class A license, or an Iowa Journeyman Class A license may reciprocate to a Minnesota Class A Journeyman license."

## Comparing the Three Pages

Read side by side, the three pages show the following:

- **The lists differ.** Iowa appears on both the Texas and Minnesota journeyman lists, and Iowa's document names both Texas and Minnesota. Minnesota does not appear on the Texas journeyman list, and Texas does not appear on Minnesota's list.
- **All three name a one-year holding period.** Texas: "Held the reciprocal license for at least one year". Minnesota: "at least one year in the first state". Iowa: "held the qualifying license for 1 year".
- **All three refer to an examination.** Texas lists: "Taken and passed the reciprocal state’s examination". Minnesota requires a license obtained "by examination at the state level". Iowa requires "an approved State-sponsored examination with a score of 70% or greater".
- **Texas adds an hours requirement:** "at least 8,000 hours under the supervision of a master electrician."
- **The fees differ:** Texas lists "License fee of $30.00". Minnesota lists "an application and examination fee of $50", with the license fee due "Upon approval".

## Licenses Obtained Through Reciprocity: One Ohio Example

The Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board's [Texas Electrical Reciprocity Application](https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/com.ohio.gov/DICO/OCILB/dico_TX_Reciprocity.pdf) covers the Master Electrician license. It states: "In order to reciprocate with Ohio for the Ohio Electrical License, the applicant must currently hold a Texas Master Electrician License for no less than 1year, and previously tested with Texas to obtain their license(s). Applicants who obtained the Texas license via Grandfathering, Reciprocity or National Test, must apply to take the Ohio exam."

That sentence applies to Ohio and Texas licenses only.

## Where to Verify

The destination state's own page is the one that governs your application. Before you apply:

1. Find your state on the destination state's current list for your license level.
2. Check the holding period and examination conditions against your own license history.
3. Ask your current board for the verification or letter of good standing the destination board asks for. Our guide on [how to request license verification](/guides/how-to-request-license-verification) covers this step.

## Questions to Ask the Destination Board Before You Apply

- Is my state on your current list for my license level?
- Did I obtain my license the way your page requires, for example by examination at the state level?
- Have I held my license for the period your page requires, and has it stayed current and in good standing?
- Have I ever taken your state's exam and failed it? Minnesota and Iowa both list this condition.
- Do you require a minimum number of supervised hours, as Texas does?
- Which fees are due with the application, and which are due after approval?
