## What This Guide Covers

This guide sets out what the Texas Board of Nursing (BON) publishes about licensure by endorsement for registered nurses educated in the United States: who is eligible, what a complete application includes, the fees, and how temporary licenses work. It draws on the BON's endorsement page, its RN endorsement instructions (version V5.2026) and its fee schedule.

It does not cover nurses educated outside the U.S. or in Puerto Rico, who use a separate application, LVN licensure, or advanced practice (APRN) licensure. It is not legal advice.

## What the Board's Own Text Says

### What endorsement means in Texas

The BON's [endorsement page](https://www.bon.texas.gov/licensure_endorsement.asp.html) quotes its rules: endorsement is "The process of issuing a permanent license without further examination to a nurse from another jurisdiction or licensing authority after determination is made that the applicant meets the same standards as those required of Texas nurses." [Rule 217.1 (12)]

### Who is eligible

The same page states: "Per Rule 217.5 (a) To be eligible for licensure by endorsement, you must have:"

- "graduated from an approved licensed practical nurse/vocational nurse (LPN/LVN) or registered nurse (RN) program"
- "taken the appropriate US exam, and"
- "practiced nursing in another state within the previous four years of applying for that license type"

The [RN endorsement instructions](https://www.bon.texas.gov/pdfs/education_pdfs/education_programs/RN%20END%20US%20INSTRUCTIONS.pdf) set out four qualifications in more detail. Among them:

- "You must have received either a passing score on the National Council Licensure Examination for Registered Nurses (NCLEX-RN) OR a score of 350 or above in the State Board Test Pool Examination (SBTPE)."
- "You must hold OR have held at least one (1) registered nurse (RN) license in another U.S. state or territory which has requirements substantially equivalent to Texas."
- "You must have practiced as a registered nurse OR have graduated from your registered nursing program within the past four (4) years from the date of your application."

If you have not practiced in the last four years, the instructions point to a Six-Month Permit under Rule 217.5 (c), and state that "you will NOT be issued a temporary or permanent license" until you complete a refresher course, an extensive orientation, or academic nursing courses.

The endorsement page also lists who cannot apply by endorsement, including anyone who has "previously held a Texas nursing license. You should either renew or reactivate the Texas license you once held."

### What a complete application includes

The instructions list "Items Required For A Completed Endorsement Application":

- The RN Endorsement Application, submitted through the Texas Nurse Portal
- "Full Endorsement Application fee"
- "Fingerprint submission for a background check"
- "Passing the Nursing Jurisprudence Exam (NJE)"
- "Verification(s) of Licensure (VRs) for ALL license(s)/authorization(s) to practice you've been issued, regardless of being current or expired"

On verifications, the instructions state: "Effective July 2025, all U.S. states and territories (excluding Puerto Rico) participate in the national database (NURSYS) for licensure verification for Endorsement." They add: "Verifications from Nursys-participating states and territories MUST be received via Nursys; verifications received by another means (mail, fax, email) will be rejected." They also require proof of your original exam license: "All endorsement applicants MUST have an exam state verification sent to the TXBON as part of their application."

On the jurisprudence exam: "The NJE is 2 hours long and based on the Texas Nursing Practice Act (NPA) and Texas Board Rules & Regulations."

### Fees

The BON's [fee page](https://www.bon.texas.gov/forms_fees.asp.html) lists "Endorsement (NJE fee Included)" at $150. Its Chapter 223 fee schedule lists "(2) Endorsement" at $125 and "(17) Nursing Jurisprudence Examination fee: not to exceed" $25. The fingerprint background check fee is listed as "Varies". The schedule states: "All fees are non-refundable."

### Temporary license and timing

The instructions state that "There is not a separate application for a temporary license" and that "The temporary license will allow you to practice as an RN in Texas for a total of 120 calendar days from the date of issuance." They add: "Temporary licenses are issued once per lifetime and are not extended or renewed for any reason regardless of how many times a person applies."

On timing, the instructions state: "Board staff aims to process applications and supplemental items within fifteen (15) business days* of the date the items are received." They also state that applications and fees "are valid for one (1) year from the date received."

If you hold a multistate license, the instructions say: "If you hold an active multi-state license, you may practice in Texas using your active multi-state license while you are in the process of fulfilling the requirements to receive a permanent Texas license." They also note: "You should only apply for a Texas license if you are moving to Texas and are changing your state of residency to Texas."

## Steps, as the Board Describes Them

1. Check the four qualifications in the instructions, including the four-year practice-or-graduation rule.
2. Apply online through the Texas Nurse Portal and pay the endorsement fee.
3. Submit fingerprints for the background check, following the instructions for applicants living in or outside Texas.
4. Request verification of every RN license you hold or have held through Nursys, selecting "Texas" as the recipient, and make sure your exam state verification is included.
5. Register for and pass the Nursing Jurisprudence Exam.
6. Complete everything within one year of the date the application is received.

## What This Guide Cannot Tell You

Confirm these with the BON before you apply:

- How the endorsement page's practice rule ("practiced nursing in another state within the previous four years") and the instructions' rule (practiced "OR have graduated" within four years) apply to your situation. The two sources word it differently.
- How long your own application will take. The fifteen business days is the board's stated aim, and the instructions note that "Processing timeframes may increase during peak seasons."
- What the fingerprint check will cost you, which the fee schedule lists as varying.
- Whether a criminal, disciplinary or health history will trigger the Enforcement Department review the instructions describe.
