## What This Guide Covers

This guide quotes four official documents, from Texas, California, Florida and Ohio, that use the word "reciprocity" for HVAC or air-conditioning licenses. It covers only what each document says. Requirements for any other state come from that state's licensing board.

## Texas: Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors

The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) licenses air conditioning and refrigeration contractors. Its [contractor license application (ACR002)](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/acr/forms/acr002%20contractor%20license%20application.pdf) has a section headed "RECIPROCITY". It states: "If you are applying for this license through reciprocity: (Texas currently reciprocates with Georgia and South Carolina only)"

The form then lists what to attach:

- "(a) a letter of good standing from the reciprocating state;"
- "(b) a copy of your current out-of-state license; and"
- "(c) a completed certificate of insurance"

The application's instructions describe the letter more fully: "a letter of good standing from South Carolina or Georgia which also indicates you have taken and passed an exam to obtain the out of state license".

## California: The C-20 Classification

The Contractors State License Board (CSLB) licenses HVAC contractors under the [C-20 Warm-Air Heating, Ventilating and Air-Conditioning Contractor](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/about_us/library/licensing_classifications/Licensing_Classifications_Detail.aspx?Class=C20) classification. CSLB describes it this way: "A warm-air heating, ventilating and air-conditioning contractor fabricates, installs, maintains, services and repairs warm-air heating systems and water heating heat pumps, complete with warm-air appliances; ventilating systems complete with blowers and plenum chambers; air-conditioning systems complete with air-conditioning unit; and the ducts, registers, flues, humidity and thermostatic controls and air filters in connection with any of these systems."

CSLB's [Reciprocity Requirements page](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Contractors/Applicants/Reciprocity/Reciprocity_Requirements.aspx) covers "Contractors in Arizona, Louisiana, Mississippi, Nevada, and North Carolina". Among its requirements:

- "The contractor must be applying for a license in a classification that appears on that state's Reciprocal Classifications List."
- "The contractor must have held an active license in good standing in one of the reciprocal states for the previous five years."

On CSLB's [Reciprocal Classifications List](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Contractors/Applicants/Reciprocity/Reciprocal_Classifications_List.aspx), on October 30, 2026, we found rows mapping to C-20 under two states:

- **Arizona:** for example, C-39 "Air Conditioning and Refrigeration, Including Solar", mapping to C-20 "Warm-air Heating, Ventilating and Air Conditioning".
- **Nevada:** C-21 "Heating Cooling & Circulating Air", "Refrigeration & Air Conditioning", mapping to C-20 "Warm-Air Heating, Ventilating & Air Conditioning".

If your license is from one of the reciprocal states, find its class code on the current list to see whether it maps to C-20.

## Florida: Reciprocity Form vs. Endorsement Form

Florida's Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB) uses two separate applications, one for reciprocity and one for endorsement.

The reciprocity form, [DBPR CILB 32](https://flrules.org/gateway/readRefFile.asp?refId=17723&filename=DBPR+CILB+32+-+Application+for+Certification+by+Reciprocity.doc), lists these license categories:

- For Louisiana or Mississippi: Certified General Contractor, Certified Building Contractor and Certified Residential Contractor.
- For North Carolina: the same three categories.

No air-conditioning category appears on CILB 32.

The endorsement form, [DBPR CILB 10](https://flrules.org/gateway/readRefFile.asp?refId=17718&filename=DBPR+CILB+10+-+Application+for+Certification+by+Endorsement_New+Final.doc), lists "Class A Air-Conditioning" and "Class B Air-Conditioning" among its categories. It describes three options under Section 489.115(3), Florida Statutes. One of them, Option C, reads: "Has held a valid, current license to practice contracting issued by another state or territory of the United States for at least 10 years before the date of the application provided that the license is currently active or was active within the last 2 years. The license type must be the same or similar as the license type being applied for."

Other details on CILB 10:

- "Fee: $350 for Endorsement application."
- "Electronic fingerprints."
- On options that compare exams: "endorsement application requirements relying on a comparison of a state specific test are very stringent and rarely approved by the Construction Industry Licensing Board."

## Ohio: West Virginia Mechanical Reciprocity

The Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board's [West Virginia Mechanical Reciprocity Application](https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/com.ohio.gov/DICO/OCILB/dico_WV_HVREPL_Reciprocity.pdf) covers HVAC and plumbing. It states: "In order to reciprocate with Ohio for an HVAC or PL License, the applicant must currently hold a West Virginia Master Plumber, or HVAC Technician and WV Contractor License. All applicants must have previously tested with West Virginia to obtain their license(s). Applicants who obtained the WV License via Grandfathering cannot reciprocate."

The form's verification section adds: "The certification must include a state seal and confirmation the license holder passed the state examination. Scores must be provided by the state you are reciprocating from."

## What the Four Documents Have in Common

Each document limits reciprocity to named states. Texas names Georgia and South Carolina. CSLB names Arizona, Louisiana, Mississippi, Nevada and North Carolina, and maps each state's classifications on a list. Florida's CILB 32 names Louisiana, Mississippi and North Carolina, for three categories. Ohio's form names West Virginia. Three of the four documents ask for evidence that the applicant passed an exam: the Texas instructions, Ohio's form and, for its exam-based option, Florida's CILB 10.

If your state is not named on the destination's document, check what other route that board offers. In Florida, CILB 10 directs applicants seeking reciprocity to CILB 32, and itself covers endorsement.
