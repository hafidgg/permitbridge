## The C-36 Plumbing Contractor Classification

In California, plumbing contractors are licensed by the Contractors State License Board (CSLB) under the C-36 Plumbing Contractor classification. CSLB's [C-36 classification page](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/about_us/library/licensing_classifications/Licensing_Classifications_Detail.aspx?Class=C36) describes the trade this way: "A plumbing contractor provides a means for a supply of safe water, ample in volume and of suitable temperature for the purpose intended and the proper disposal of fluid waste from the premises in all structures and fixed works."

The same page lists work the classification includes. Three examples from that list:

- "Complete removal of waste from the premises or the construction and connection of on-site waste disposal systems"
- "Piping, storage tanks and venting for a safe and adequate supply of gases and liquids for any purpose, including vacuum, compressed air and gases for medical, dental, commercial and industrial uses"
- "All gas appliances, flues and gas connections for all systems including suspended space heating units. This does not include forced warm air units"

## The Application and the Qualifier

CSLB's page for [sole owner applicants](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Contractors/Applicants/Apply_for_a_Sole_Owner_License/default.aspx) says its "Application for Original Contractor License" "is for new applicants for Sole Owner entity types, when experience and examinations are required." The page also states that "Each license requires a “qualifier”" and that "Only one A, B, or C level classification may be applied for with this application."

The same page states: "There is no educational requirement to apply for or act as the qualifier on a license."

## Experience: Four Years Within the Last Ten

According to the sole owner page, the qualifier must have "four years within the last ten years" of work experience "in the trade being applied for". The page lists the roles that count: journeyperson, foreperson, supervising employee, contractor and owner-builder.

Two of the page's definitions matter most to plumbers:

- "Journeyperson: An experienced worker who is fully qualified, as opposed to a trainee, and who can perform the trade without supervision; or someone who has completed an apprenticeship program."
- "Contractor: Someone with a current or previous contractor license, either in California or in another state."

The owner-builder role is defined as "Someone who performs B-General Building classification work solely on their own property", so it relates to the B-General Building classification.

Experience is documented on a separate form. The sole owner page states: "A Certification of Work Experience form must be used to report and confirm the journey-level work experience requirement. This form must be submitted with the application and can be found on the CSLB website." The [Certification of Work Experience form](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/FormsAndApplications/CertificationOfWorkExperience.pdf) defines a contractor as "an individual who is currently a licensed California contractor, a former licensed California contractor, or an out-of-state licensed contractor."

## Exams

The sole owner page describes the exams in this order:

- "After CSLB accepts the application, the qualifier will need to pass a trade exam and/or a business and law exam"
- "Examinations are administered by an approved testing vendor and fees for the examinations are paid directly to the vendor by the applicant."
- "Information about scheduling the examination(s) will be mailed once the application is accepted by CSLB."
- "Free exam study guides are available on CSLB's website"

CSLB does not state the exam fee amounts on that page, because they are paid to the vendor.

After the exams are passed, the page lists these "Additional Requirements for License Issuance":

- "Contractor bond."
- "Workers’ compensation insurance or exemption."
- "Asbestos open book exam."

## Fees for a Sole Owner

The amounts below are the ones CSLB states for a **sole owner** license. Other entity types have different fees, so check CSLB's fee list for those.

- **Application:** "Non-refundable application fee of $450 due as well as a 2.3% fee added by the credit card company when this form is submitted online." CSLB's [fee list](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/About_Us/Library/Fees.aspx) shows the same $450 for "Original Application (exam or waiver for one classification)".
- **Initial license:** "Initial license fee of $200 for a sole owner is due after the qualifier passes the exam(s)." The page adds: "If approved for the military expedite and initial license fee reduction provision, these initial license fees will be reduced by 50%."
- **Renewal:** "Renewal fee of $450 for a sole owner license will be due every two years after licensure."

For comparison, the fee list shows "Initial License Fee (Non-Sole Owner)" at $350.

## Reciprocity: Which States, and On What Terms

CSLB's [Reciprocity Requirements page](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Contractors/Applicants/Reciprocity/Reciprocity_Requirements.aspx) is headed "Contractors in Arizona, Louisiana, Mississippi, Nevada, and North Carolina". It says "Reciprocity requirements are the same for each state" and lists them:

1. "The contractor must be applying for a license in a classification that appears on that state's Reciprocal Classifications List."
2. "The contractor must have held an active license in good standing in one of the reciprocal states for the previous five years."
3. "The contractor must submit to CSLB the Request for Verification of License form that is completed by the licensing entity under which they are already licensed."
4. "The contractor must complete the Application for Original Contractor's License."

The first requirement depends on CSLB's [Reciprocal Classifications List](https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Contractors/Applicants/Reciprocity/Reciprocal_Classifications_List.aspx). It explains how to read the list: "The column to the left includes the license class codes and category descriptions for each state. The column to the right lists the corresponding license classification for California." Its own example is: "For example, a C-7 Carpentry license in Arizona corresponds to a C-5 Framing and Rough Carpentry license in California."

On October 30, 2026, the rows mapping to C-36 that we found on that list were all under Arizona, for example Arizona's C-37 "Plumbing, Including Solar". The list's own introduction names the states differently from the Requirements page: "The following are the Reciprocal License Classifications for California and three other states: Arizona, Louisiana, Nevada, or North Carolina." Check the current list for your state and classification before you apply.

CSLB has a second reciprocity route for contractors who have passed the NASCLA Commercial General Builders Examination. Its first requirement is: "The contractor is applying for the CSLB General Building classification." That route is for General Building, not C-36.

The page also covers the opposite direction: "If a California contractor wants to apply for reciprocity in another state, they must ask that state for its reciprocity form and have it forwarded to CSLB."

## How to Use This Guide

1. Start with the classification section. Check that your work matches the C-36 scope as CSLB describes it.
2. If you hold a license from one of the states named on the Reciprocity Requirements page, read the reciprocity section first. Otherwise, read the experience, exam and fee sections in order.
3. Gather records of your work in each role before you fill in the Certification of Work Experience form.
4. Open each CSLB page from the Sources list and check it against what this guide quotes. Rely on the page if the two differ.

## Questions to Ask CSLB Before You Apply

- Which of the listed roles does my experience fall under: journeyperson, foreperson, supervising employee, contractor or owner-builder?
- Does all of my experience fall within the period CSLB counts?
- Does my out-of-state contractor license count as contractor experience on the Certification of Work Experience form?
- Will I take the trade exam, the business and law exam, or both?
- If I apply by reciprocity, does my state's class code appear on the Reciprocal Classifications List with C-36 as the California classification?
- Has my license been active and in good standing for the whole period the Reciprocity Requirements page names?
- If I am not applying as a sole owner, which fees apply to my entity type?
