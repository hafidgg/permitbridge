## What This Guide Covers

The Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board (OCILB) publishes a separate reciprocity application for each state it has an agreement with. This guide sets out what nine of those forms say: Alabama, Arkansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas and West Virginia. For each one it covers who the form says can apply, which trades the form lists, and what the applicant must still send.

It does not cover the plain out-of-state examination route in full, local (city or county) licenses, or residential work. It also cannot tell you whether OCILB has agreements with states not listed here: OCILB's licensing web page did not return readable content when we checked, so this guide relies only on the forms themselves.

## What the Board's Own Text Says

OCILB's [Out of State Examination Application](https://dam.assets.ohio.gov/image/upload/com.ohio.gov/DICO/OCILB/Out_of_State_Testing_Application_-_OCILB.pdf) describes the board this way: "The Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board (OCILB), issues state licenses to Electrical, HVAC, Refrigeration, Plumbing, and Hydronics Contractors who perform commercial work."

### What every reciprocity form asks for

All nine forms list the same required documents and fee:

- Insurance: "Certificate of Liability Insurance Form, including without limit, complete operations coverage in the amount of at least $500,000".
- "Verification of Current Reciprocating State License".
- A contracting company: "In accordance with Section 4740.01 of the Ohio Revised Code (ORC), an individual is required to assign their license to a Contracting Company."
- The fee: "$25.00 non-refundable fee per trade."

Seven of the forms (Arkansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, South Carolina, Tennessee and West Virginia) include a "Reciprocity Verification of Licensure" page for the other state's board to complete. It states: "The certification must include a state seal and confirmation the license holder passed the state examination. Scores must be provided by the state you are reciprocating from." The Alabama form instead asks you to "Request and submit a completed and signed Certification Request Form" from the Alabama Heating, Air Conditioning & Refrigeration Contractors Board. The Texas form asks for "a completed and signed letter of good standing" from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR).

### State by state

The forms label their trade checkboxes EL, HVAC, RE, HY and PL. The forms do not define these labels; the examination application lists OCILB's trades as Electrical, HVAC, Refrigeration, Plumbing and Hydronics.

| State | Trades on the form | Who the form says can reciprocate |
|---|---|---|
| Alabama | HVAC, RE, EL | Holds an Alabama license and "previously tested with Alabama" |
| Arkansas | Plumbing | The form gives no eligibility paragraph |
| Kentucky | EL, HVAC, RE | For electrical: a "Kentucky Master Electrician and Contractor License"; all applicants must have tested |
| Louisiana | EL, HVAC, RE, HY | A "Louisiana Unlimited Electrical License or Mechanical License", obtained by testing |
| Mississippi | EL, HVAC, RE, PL | A Mississippi license held "for no less than 1year", obtained by testing |
| South Carolina | EL, HVAC, PL | A South Carolina license obtained by testing |
| Tennessee | EL, HVAC, RE, HY, PL | A Tennessee license obtained by testing or grandfathering |
| Texas | Electrical | A "Texas Master Electrician License for no less than 1year", obtained by testing |
| West Virginia | HVAC, PL | A "West Virginia Master Plumber, or HVAC Technician and WV Contractor License", obtained by testing |

### Licenses obtained without a test

The forms treat licenses obtained by grandfathering or reciprocity differently, and the wording matters:

- **Alabama, Louisiana, Mississippi and South Carolina:** applicants who obtained the license "via Grandfathering or Reciprocity must apply to take the Ohio exam, and are required to take Ohio Business and Law exam."
- **Kentucky and West Virginia:** applicants who obtained the license via grandfathering "cannot reciprocate."
- **Tennessee:** "Applicants who obtained the Tennessee license via Reciprocity from another state are not permitted to Reciprocate into Ohio. Applicants will be required to take the Ohio Business and Law exam."
- **Texas:** "Applicants who obtained the Texas license via Grandfathering, Reciprocity or National Test, must apply to take the Ohio exam."

The Alabama, Mississippi and South Carolina forms add: "Each trade requires one application and one fee per trade." The Kentucky and Louisiana forms say each trade requires "a separate application and fee."

### If your state has no agreement form

The Out of State Examination Application sets out a separate route. For "an individual who holds an out-of-state trades contractors license, as defined in section 4796.01 ORC, that is substantially similar to the license for which the individual is applying", it asks for proof of "at least five authorizations for construction" from a building authority and "at least one tax return that reflects income earned for services provided under the individuals out-of-state occupational license", among other items. After passing, the form says a license is issued on "a copy of the examination results, a $25 check made payable to 'Treasurer, State of Ohio', and proof of at least $500,000 contractor liability insurance".

## Steps, as the Forms Describe Them

1. Find the form for the state that issued your license and check that your license type matches the form's wording, including any minimum holding period (one year on the Mississippi and Texas forms).
2. Confirm how you obtained the license. If it was by grandfathering or reciprocity, read the form's sentence on that, because it may mean taking the Ohio exam or rule out reciprocity entirely.
3. Ask your current board to complete the verification the form names: the verification page, Alabama's Certification Request Form, or TDLR's letter of good standing.
4. Arrange the liability insurance certificate of at least $500,000. Every form states that the "certificate Holder must reflect: Ohio Construction Licensing Board, 6606 Tussing Road, Reynoldsburg, OH, 43068."
5. Name the contracting company your license will be assigned to.
6. Send one application and the $25.00 fee for each trade.

## What This Guide Cannot Tell You

Confirm these with OCILB before you apply:

- Whether OCILB has reciprocity agreements with any state not listed here.
- How long OCILB takes to process a reciprocity application. None of the forms states a processing time.
- Whether the Ohio Business and Law exam applies to Arkansas, Kentucky, Texas or West Virginia applicants. Those forms do not mention it.
- What the EL, HVAC, RE, HY and PL checkboxes cover exactly, since the forms do not define them.
- Whether your specific license class (for example, a journeyman rather than a master license) is accepted.
