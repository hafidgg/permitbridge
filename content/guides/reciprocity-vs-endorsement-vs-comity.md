## Three Terms Boards Use

Licensing boards describe their routes for out-of-state license holders with different words: "reciprocity," "endorsement," and sometimes "comity." States do not all use these words the same way, so the definitions below are general. The receiving board's own rules decide what applies to you.

## Reciprocity

Reciprocity usually means the receiving state recognizes licenses from specific states it has an agreement with. For example, the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation lists the states it has [reciprocal licensing agreements](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/electricians/out-of-state.htm) with for Master and Journeyman Electricians. A license from a state that is not on such a list does not qualify under that agreement.

## Endorsement

Licensure by endorsement usually means the receiving state reviews an existing license from another state against its own requirements and issues its own license. It does not depend on an agreement between the two states, and the requirements can still include an exam, coursework or a fee. New York, for example, licenses registered nurses from other states [by endorsement](https://www.op.nysed.gov/professions/registered-professional-nursing/endorsement-nursing-licenses).

## Comity

Some states use "comity" for recognizing a license issued elsewhere. What it requires depends on that state's own law and rules.

## What This Means for Your Application

The route your destination state uses decides what you need to submit and whether an exam applies. Use the exact name your destination board gives its route, and confirm the requirements with that board before you apply.
